# JLPT N4 Vocabulary-First Kanji Override Proposals

**Status**: Proposal Only - Not Yet Implemented
**Created**: 2026-03-24
**Revised**: 2026-03-24 (after review feedback)
**Scope**: 6 required overrides + 3 optional safeguards across 5 N4 kanji
**Methodology**: Systematic review of jmdict-eng-common.json against CURATION_RULES.md

---

## Overview

This document proposes curated vocabulary overrides for N4 kanji where the heuristic ranking produces pedagogically weak picks. These proposals follow the standards in `CURATION_RULES.md` and prioritize:

1. Everyday usefulness over technical terminology
2. **Meaning transparency** (kanji contribution visible to learner)
3. Reading clarity (easy to associate with target kanji)
4. Short, simple forms over complex compounds
5. Learner-friendly orthography

**Override Philosophy**:
- `required` = Heuristic pick is clearly pedagogically bad and needs correction
- `optional` = Safeguard or preference refinement; heuristic likely picks correctly
- Overrides should be **sparse and high-value** - not every reasonable suggestion needs to be hard-coded

## Required Overrides

These overrides address clear pedagogical weaknesses in likely heuristic picks.

### 1. 度 (degree, times)

**Kanji**: 度
**JLPT**: N4
**Reading Type**: onyomi
**Reading**: ど
**Status**: **required**
**Current Heuristic Concern**: Heuristic likely picks 温度 (temperature) which is technical/scientific vocabulary
**Proposed Word**: 何度
**Proposed Word Reading**: なんど
**Proposed Meaning**: how many times; how many degrees
**Reason**: Much more common in everyday conversation than 温度. Learners use this constantly ("何度も" = many times, "何度も言った" = I said it many times). Temperature reading is domain-specific (science/weather). 何度 also works for asking temperature but in natural contexts.
**Confidence**: high

### 2. 歩 (walk, steps)

**Kanji**: 歩
**JLPT**: N4
**Reading Type**: onyomi
**Reading**: ホ
**Status**: **required**
**Current Heuristic Concern**: Multiple on'yomi readings (ホ, フ, ブ) with different compounds. Heuristic might pick formal/abstract compounds like 初歩 (basics) or 進歩 (progress).
**Proposed Word**: 散歩
**Proposed Word Reading**: さんぽ
**Proposed Meaning**: walk, stroll
**Reason**: Most common on'yomi compound for everyday use. Concrete activity word that N4 learners encounter frequently in conversation and textbooks. Clear semantic pattern (散 = scatter/leisurely + 歩 = walk). Much more useful than abstract alternatives.
**Confidence**: high

## Removed After Review

The following proposals were in the initial draft but have been removed after critical review:

### Removed: 赤 + あか → 赤ちゃん

**Why Removed**: Violates **meaning transparency** principle. The connection between 赤 (red) and 赤ちゃん (baby) is semantically opaque for beginners - learners cannot reasonably understand what "red" contributes to "baby". The word is very common, but this does not override the lack of pedagogical clarity for teaching the kanji's core meaning.

**Better approach**: Let heuristic pick 赤い (red adjective) or similar transparent forms. If needed, this could be an optional override to prefer 赤い over 赤ちゃん, but forcing 赤ちゃん is pedagogically weak for **kanji study**.

---

### Removed: 赤 + セキ → 赤外線

**Why Removed**: Violates **avoid technical terminology** principle. 赤外線 (infrared rays) is technical/scientific vocabulary inappropriate for first-exposure N4 teaching. Just because it's the only common option doesn't justify hard-coding a technical term.

**Better approach**: Leave to heuristic fallback. If no good common example exists, that's acceptable - not every reading needs a forced override.

---

### Removed: 代 + タイ → 代謝

**Why Removed**: Violates **avoid technical terminology** principle. 代謝 (metabolism) is biological/medical vocabulary. Being marked "common" in dictionary doesn't make it pedagogically appropriate for N4 kanji study first exposure.

**Better approach**: Leave to heuristic fallback or embedded examples. No override needed if no good everyday alternative exists.

---

### Removed: 色 + いろ → 赤色

**Why Removed**: Weak justification. The standalone 色 (いろ = color) is perfectly adequate for teaching the kun'yomi reading. While 赤色 (red color) is valid, it's not **clearly better** than the base word for first exposure. The override doesn't solve a pedagogical problem.

**Better approach**: Let heuristic pick. If heuristic already picks 色 or a color compound, that's fine. No strong need for override.
